A remarkable local resource

From the beach to the museum.

As a Shell Ambassador for the Bailey-Matthews National Shell Museum & Aquarium, I enjoy connecting beachgoers with the living animals behind the shells they encounter. Sanibel is often called the Seashell Capital of the World, and we are fortunate to have a museum dedicated to mollusks and their ecosystems right here in Southwest Florida.

Research collaboration · FSW × Shell Museum

From photographs to digital specimens.

Dr. Sauer at Florida SouthWestern State College, José H. Leal at the Bailey-Matthews National Shell Museum & Aquarium, and FSW student research assistants connect shell collections with digital imaging and research through photogrammetry. Overlapping photographs are processed into interactive three-dimensional models that can be examined from multiple angles.

Explore this example of Forreria belcheri, credited to the FSW STEM collection. Digital specimens offer opportunities to investigate morphology, compare structures and develop quantitative research questions. Measurements require appropriate calibration and validation.

Digital malacology · Exploratory research

How different are two shells in three dimensions?

Explore comparative horse conch morphology through interactive 3D specimens and a coordinate-based landmark alignment workspace. This prototype introduces quantitative shape comparison and outlines a pathway toward validated surface registration and species-level research.

Science in the classroom

Learning classification through real shells.

I incorporate shell identification into biology classification laboratory activities, connecting the terminology students learn with real biological diversity. Shell shape, symmetry, sculpture, aperture and hinge features provide observable evidence for comparing molluscan groups and practicing identification.
