How to use this laboratory

Compare the shape of two real shells in three dimensions, rather than comparing only their photographs. No specialized software installation is required.

01 · Prepare your scans

Obtain two original 3D surface meshes in OBJ, PLY (with triangle faces), STL or self-contained GLB format. Record the species, specimen accession number, physical dimensions and scan units. Use complete, clean meshes with comparable coverage; remove stands, backgrounds and unrelated geometry before analysis. Each file must be under 100 MB and contain no more than 500,000 triangles.

02 · Select your specimens

Choose specimen A as the reference and specimen B as the moving specimen. The reference remains fixed; the algorithm moves B to match A. Use the same calibrated units for both files. The Sketchfab viewers further down this page are for visual exploration only; they do not automatically import geometry for measurement.

03 · Choose your scale

Preserve original dimensions: compare absolute geometry, including size differences. If both scans use millimeters, the results are in millimeters. Match RMS surface radius: rescale B uniformly to the approximate overall size of A to focus on shape. Results remain in reference-mesh units, not dimensionless units. This does not remove allometric effects.

04 · Run automatic alignment

Select “Align and calculate deviations.” The algorithm centers the specimens, tests 24 starting orientations and uses iterative closest point (ICP) to refine a fit by matching sampled points on B to the nearest triangle surfaces on A. Progress is displayed while it runs. Large models may take considerable time; keep the tab open.

05 · Inspect the heat map

Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, and enable “Overlay reference wireframe” to see A over the aligned B surface. Check that the apex, aperture, body whorl and siphonal canal are oriented sensibly. Blue indicates smaller distances and red indicates larger distances; neither indicates a direction of change. The red end is capped at a data-derived 95th-percentile threshold, so compare numerical scales between runs rather than colors alone.

06 · Save your results

Read the numerical results for both directions, B to A and A to B. Select “Export results (JSON)” to save the filenames, scale factor, transformation matrix, summary measurements and sampled coordinates with distances. Keep this file with your original meshes and specimen metadata for reproducibility.

What do the numbers mean?

Mean distance
Average unsigned distance from sampled points to the other mesh surface.
RMS distance
Root mean square distance; gives greater weight to large deviations.
95th percentile (P95)
Approximately 95% of sampled distances are at or below this value.
Sampled maximum
Largest distance among the 5,000 sampled points in that direction; not a guaranteed maximum over the entire surface.
Bidirectional distances
A-to-B and B-to-A may differ, especially when one shell has missing regions or additional structures. The displayed heat map represents B-to-A vertex distances.
Scale factor and transform
The factor applied to B and its final affine transformation, stored as a column-major 4 × 4 matrix in the export.

Example interpretation (hypothetical)

If calibrated scans produce an RMS distance of 0.8 mm and a P95 of 1.9 mm, most sampled surface distances are within approximately 1.9 mm after alignment. These are illustrative numbers, not results from horse conch specimens. A low distance does not prove two specimens are the same species.

Research quality and troubleshooting

Automatic registration may settle in a local optimum or match nonhomologous structures. Shells with different coiling, damage, incomplete apertures or scan artifacts are especially challenging. If alignment looks incorrect, orient copies of both meshes consistently in external software, check units and remove artifacts before rerunning. Use manual anatomical landmarks as an independent comparison. Validate results against trusted software such as CloudCompare before publication. Multiple specimens per species, repeat scans and appropriate statistical analysis are necessary for species-level conclusions.

Privacy: the local mesh analysis reads files in browser memory and does not intentionally transmit geometry to a server. Internet access is required for the hosted libraries and optional Sketchfab viewers. If the analysis fails to initialize, check WebGL support, network access and file format; try a smaller triangulated mesh on a desktop browser.

Landmark comparison workspace
Use this independent tool when you have manually recorded corresponding anatomical XYZ coordinates. Enter one landmark per line for each specimen, in the same order; for example, apex, a defined shoulder point, and siphonal canal tip. The first three points define the alignment frame and must be distinct and non-collinear. Select normalization if you want to compare centroid-size-normalized coordinates. Click “Align and compare landmarks,” inspect the residual distances, and export the CSV. This tool does not automatically extract landmarks from either mesh or Sketchfab.
Enter corresponding XYZ landmarks from calibrated scans in the same anatomical order. Use at least three non-collinear points. This prototype performs a three-point anatomical frame alignment, optionally normalized by centroid size, and reports landmark RMS deviation. It is not a full Procrustes fit or surface registration.
 
Research protocol
1. Acquire
Obtain original OBJ, PLY, STL or GLB files, accession identifiers, verified taxonomy and physical scale.
2. Landmark
Define repeatable landmarks at the apex, shoulder, aperture and siphonal canal. Use homologous points and document repeatability.
3. Register
Compare calibrated and size-normalized geometry; validate full surface registration in CloudCompare or SlicerMorph.
4. Evaluate
Use multiple specimens per species and size class, assess allometry and perform geometric morphometric statistics in R.
Proposed comparison
