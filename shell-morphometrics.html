Automatic 3D alignment & surface deviation

Choose two local triangulated meshes (OBJ, PLY, STL or self-contained GLB). Geometry stays in your browser; software libraries load from a CDN. Texture data is not used. Maximum 100 MB and 500,000 triangles per file. Use desktop hardware for large scans.

Unsigned nearest-surface distance: blue = close, red = far.

Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom. The numerical summary provides a nonvisual alternative. Multistart point-to-surface ICP tests 24 orientations, refines the best fit and calculates bidirectional distances to triangle surfaces. The displayed heat map is B-to-A vertex distance; summary statistics use area-distributed samples. This is an exploratory method, not validated species classification. Inspect registration, especially on spiral or incomplete shells; automatic alignment can converge to an incorrect orientation. Actual-size analysis requires consistent calibrated units; GLB may use meters. Surface distances are unsigned and do not identify anatomical correspondence.

Landmark comparison workspace
Enter corresponding XYZ landmarks from calibrated scans in the same anatomical order. Use at least three non-collinear points. This prototype performs a three-point anatomical frame alignment, optionally normalized by centroid size, and reports landmark RMS deviation. It is not a full Procrustes fit or surface registration.
 
Research protocol
1. Acquire
Obtain original OBJ, PLY, STL or GLB files, accession identifiers, verified taxonomy and physical scale.
2. Landmark
Define repeatable landmarks at the apex, shoulder, aperture and siphonal canal. Use homologous points and document repeatability.
3. Register
Compare calibrated and size-normalized geometry; validate full surface registration in CloudCompare or SlicerMorph.
4. Evaluate
Use multiple specimens per species and size class, assess allometry and perform geometric morphometric statistics in R.
Proposed comparison
Florida horse conch (Triplofusus giganteus) and Prince horse conch (Triplofusus princeps) are candidate taxa. No species-level conclusions or measured results are presented here.
